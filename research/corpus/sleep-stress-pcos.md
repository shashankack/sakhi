# Sleep, stress and PCOS

## Plain-language explanation

**Sleep**: the NHS says most healthy adults need **around 7 to 9 hours** of sleep a night. The NHS lists signs of sleep problems as finding it hard to fall asleep, lying awake for long periods, waking up several times in the night, or waking up early and not being able to get back to sleep.

**Stress**: stress can affect sleep, mood, and periods. The NHS lists **stress and anxiety** among causes of irregular periods.

## PCOS education context

The 2023 international guideline says women with PCOS have a **higher rate of obstructive sleep apnoea**, and should be asked about its symptoms. The NHS lists sleep apnoea symptoms as:

- loud snoring
- breathing stopping and starting during sleep
- waking up a lot
- feeling very tired during the day

The NHS lists feeling very tired, and depression or anxiety, among the symptoms of PMOS (the new name for PCOS). WHO also links PCOS with anxiety and depression.

This information is educational. It is not a diagnosis.

## Other possible causes and overlaps

- **Iron deficiency anaemia** can cause tiredness (NHS). See `anaemia-and-periods.md`.
- **Thyroid problems** can cause tiredness or poor sleep (NHS). See `overlap-thyroid-prolactin.md`.
- **PMS** can cause tiredness or trouble sleeping before a period (NHS). See `pms-pmdd.md`.
- **Depression and anxiety**. See `mood-fatigue.md`.

## Self-care notes

**Sleep tips from the NHS:**
- Go to bed and get up at **similar times** each day.
- Try not to use **phones, tablets, or computers** for an hour before bed.
- Tea, coffee, and some soft drinks contain **caffeine**, which can make you feel more awake. The NHS advises avoiding caffeine, smoking, and alcohol for at least 6 hours before bed.
- Some people find **exercising close to bedtime** affects sleep; try being active earlier in the day.
- A room that is **quiet, dark, and cool** may help.

**Stress tips from the NHS:**
- be active
- take control of what you can
- connect with people
- have some "me time"
- challenge yourself, and help other people
- try to be positive, and accept the things you cannot change
- do not rely on **alcohol, smoking, or caffeine** as ways of coping

## Care pathway

- **Routine**: ongoing sleep problems or stress affecting daily life → **GP**.
- **Soon**: the NHS advises seeing a GP for sleep apnoea symptoms, such as always feeling very tired during the day. Bringing someone who has seen your symptoms at night can help.
- **Urgent**: thoughts of self-harm or suicide → **Tele-MANAS 14416** or **1800-89-14416**; dial **108** if someone has seriously harmed themselves. See `red-flags-when-urgent.md`.

## Questions for a clinician

- Could my tiredness be from poor sleep, sleep apnoea, anaemia, or thyroid?
- Could I have sleep apnoea, given my snoring?
- What can I do to sleep better?
- What support is available for stress or anxiety?
