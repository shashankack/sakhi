# Overlaps: thyroid, prolactin, and other look-alikes

## Plain-language explanation

Many symptoms people link with PCOS, such as irregular periods, tiredness, weight change, hair fall, and mood changes, can also come from **other hormone conditions**. Two common ones are **thyroid problems** and **high prolactin**. Both can be checked with blood tests.

### Underactive thyroid (hypothyroidism)

The NHS lists these symptoms:
- feeling extremely tired
- feeling cold more than usual
- putting on weight
- dry skin, dry hair, or hair loss
- irregular or heavy periods

The NHS says blood tests can check levels of **TSH** (thyroid-stimulating hormone) and **T4** (thyroxine).

### Overactive thyroid (hyperthyroidism)

The NHS lists these symptoms:
- nervousness, anxiety, and irritability
- sensitivity to heat
- weight loss
- an irregular and/or unusually fast heart rate
- swelling in the neck from an enlarged thyroid gland (goitre)

### High prolactin

MedlinePlus says **prolactin** is a hormone made by the pituitary gland, a small gland at the base of the brain. Its main role is to prepare the breasts for making milk.

MedlinePlus lists these symptoms of high prolactin in women:
- changes in periods (irregular periods or no periods)
- making breast milk when not pregnant or breastfeeding
- nipple discharge
- infertility

MedlinePlus lists these causes of high prolactin:
- a **prolactinoma**, a benign (noncancerous) tumour on the pituitary gland, which it says is the most common cause
- certain medicines
- certain health conditions, such as underactive thyroid

MedlinePlus says small increases in prolactin may be caused by stress, exercise, or even having sex. MedlinePlus lists **headache** and **vision changes** among the symptoms of a prolactinoma.

## PCOS education context

WHO says PCOS is diagnosed only **after other causes are ruled out**. The NHS lists thyroid and other medical conditions, as well as PMOS (the new name for PCOS), among the causes of missed periods. So an article about PCOS is a starting point for learning, never a conclusion.

This information is educational. It is not a diagnosis.

## Other possible causes and overlaps

Other causes that sources mention for PCOS-like symptoms:

- **pregnancy**, a common reason for a missed period (NHS)
- **Cushing's syndrome** and **acromegaly**, hormone conditions that the NHS lists as rare causes of hirsutism
- rarely, a **tumour** affecting hormone levels (NHS)
- **stress, big weight changes, or exercising too much** (NHS)
- **perimenopause**, usually between ages 45 and 55 (NHS)
- **iron deficiency anaemia**, which can cause tiredness and hair loss (NHS). See `anaemia-and-periods.md`.
- **certain medicines**, such as some epilepsy medicines and steroids (NHS, for hirsutism)

## Self-care notes

- Make a **list of all medicines and supplements** you take, including herbal or ayurvedic products, to share with a clinician.
- Note **all** your symptoms, even ones that seem unrelated, such as feeling cold, a racing heart, or nipple discharge.
- Do not start thyroid or hormone medicines without medical advice.

## Care pathway

- **Routine**: symptoms that might suggest thyroid or prolactin changes → **GP** or **gynaecologist**, who may arrange blood tests and refer to an **endocrinologist**.
- **Soon**: nipple discharge when not breastfeeding, especially with **headaches or vision changes**; a fast heart rate with weight loss; thick hair appearing suddenly with a lower voice.
- **Urgent**: chest pain, fainting, or severe difficulty breathing. See `red-flags-when-urgent.md`.

## Questions for a clinician

- Could a thyroid or prolactin problem explain my symptoms?
- Should I have a pregnancy test, a thyroid test (TSH), or a prolactin test?
- Could any of my medicines be affecting my hormones or periods?
- If a result is borderline, should it be repeated?
- Do I need to see an endocrinologist?
