# Overlaps: thyroid, prolactin, and other look-alikes

## Plain-language explanation

Many symptoms people link with PCOS, such as irregular periods, tiredness, weight change, hair fall, and mood changes, can also come from **other hormone conditions**. The two most common look-alikes are **thyroid problems** and **high prolactin**. Both are usually checked with simple blood tests and are often very treatable.

### Thyroid

The thyroid is a small gland in the neck that sets the body's "speed".

- **Underactive thyroid (hypothyroidism)** may cause: tiredness, feeling cold, weight gain, constipation, dry skin, hair thinning, low mood, and heavier or irregular periods.
- **Overactive thyroid (hyperthyroidism)** may cause: weight loss despite eating well, a fast or pounding heartbeat, feeling hot and sweaty, shaky hands, anxiety, poor sleep, and lighter or irregular periods.
- Some people notice a swelling at the front of the neck (goitre).

### Prolactin

Prolactin is a hormone from the pituitary gland in the brain. It helps make breast milk.

- **High prolactin (hyperprolactinaemia)** may cause: irregular or missing periods, **milky discharge from the nipples when not breastfeeding**, difficulty conceiving, and lower sex drive.
- Causes include some medicines, an underactive thyroid, stress, and sometimes a small, usually non-cancerous growth in the pituitary gland.
- **Headaches or changes in vision** together with these symptoms should be checked **soon**.

## PCOS education context

Before using the word PCOS, clinicians usually **check for these look-alikes first**, because they can cause similar symptoms and need different care. It is also possible to have **more than one** condition at the same time, for example PCOS and a thyroid problem.

So an article about PCOS is a starting point for education. It is never a conclusion.

This information is educational. It is not a diagnosis.

## Other possible causes and overlaps

Other conditions clinicians may consider, depending on symptoms:

- **Pregnancy**: always considered with a missed period
- **Non-classic congenital adrenal hyperplasia (NCCAH)**: an inherited adrenal condition that can cause extra hair growth and irregular periods
- **Cushing's syndrome** (too much cortisol, rare): weight gain around the middle, rounder face, purple stretch marks, easy bruising, muscle weakness
- **Hormone-producing growths** (rare): very rapid hair growth, deepening voice
- **Low body weight, under-eating, or intense exercise** ("hypothalamic" cause): periods stop because the body is conserving energy
- **Perimenopause / early menopause**: hot flushes, night sweats, periods becoming irregular
- **Anaemia / low iron**: tiredness, hair fall, breathlessness
- **Diabetes**: thirst, frequent urination, tiredness
- **Medicines**: some antipsychotics, antidepressants, anti-nausea drugs, and steroids can affect hormones

## Self-care notes

- Make a **list of all medicines and supplements** you take (including ayurvedic or herbal products) to share with a clinician.
- Note **all** your symptoms, even ones that seem unrelated, such as feeling cold, heart racing, or nipple discharge.
- Do not start iodine, thyroid, or hormone supplements without medical advice.
- If you already take thyroid medicine, keep your follow-up blood tests as advised.

## Care pathway

- **Routine (next few weeks)**: symptoms suggesting thyroid or prolactin changes → **GP** or **gynaecologist**, who may arrange blood tests and refer to an **endocrinologist**.
- **Soon (within days)**: milky nipple discharge **with headaches or vision changes**; fast heartbeat with weight loss and shakiness; **rapid** hair growth with voice deepening.
- **Urgent**: chest pain, fainting, severe breathlessness. See `red-flags-when-urgent.md`.

## Questions for a clinician

- Could a thyroid or prolactin problem explain my symptoms?
- Should I have a pregnancy test, thyroid test (TSH), or prolactin test?
- Could any of my medicines be affecting my hormones or periods?
- Is it possible to have more than one hormone condition at once?
- If a test is borderline, should it be repeated?
- Do I need to see an endocrinologist?
