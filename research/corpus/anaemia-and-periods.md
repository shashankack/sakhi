# Anaemia and periods

## Plain-language explanation

WHO describes **anaemia** as a condition where the number of red blood cells, or the haemoglobin in them, is lower than normal.

WHO says **30% of women aged 15 to 49** are affected by anaemia worldwide. It says anaemia affects **244 million women** in South-East Asia.

The NHS lists these symptoms of **iron deficiency anaemia**:

- tiredness and lack of energy
- shortness of breath
- heart palpitations (a noticeable heartbeat)
- paler skin
- headaches

WHO also lists dizziness or light-headedness and cold hands and feet.

## PCOS education context

The NHS says **heavy periods and pregnancy are very common causes** of iron deficiency anaemia. WHO lists consistent heavy menstrual losses among the causes of anaemia.

WHO says some women with PCOS (now also called PMOS) have heavy periods. See `heavy-periods.md`.

Anaemia is **not** a feature used to diagnose PCOS. Tiredness and hair fall have many causes, and anaemia is one that can be checked with a blood test.

This information is educational. It is not a diagnosis.

## Other possible causes and overlaps

- **Low iron in the diet**: WHO says iron deficiency, mainly from not enough iron in the diet, is the most common nutritional cause of anaemia.
- **Pregnancy** (NHS).
- **Bleeding in the stomach or gut**, sometimes linked to anti-inflammatory painkillers such as ibuprofen and aspirin (NHS).
- **Underactive thyroid** can also cause tiredness and hair loss (NHS).

## Self-care notes

- **Eat iron-rich foods.** The NHS lists dark-green leafy vegetables, fortified cereals and bread, meat, dried fruit, and pulses. ICMR-NIN says green leafy vegetables, pulses, and dry fruits contain iron.
- **Add vitamin C.** ICMR-NIN says vitamin C-rich fruits such as guava, pineapple, and citrus fruits (lemon, orange) improve iron absorption from plant foods.
- **Watch tea timing.** ICMR-NIN says tea binds iron, and advises avoiding tea before, during, or soon after a meal. The NHS says tea, coffee, milk, and dairy can make it harder to absorb iron.
- Do not start iron tablets on your own. A blood test helps decide what is needed.

## Care pathway

- **Routine**: the NHS advises seeing a GP if you think you might have iron deficiency anaemia. The NHS says a GP usually orders a **full blood count (FBC)** test.
- **Soon**: tiredness, breathlessness, or paler skin together with heavy periods.
- **Urgent**: heavy bleeding with feeling faint or dizzy, or chest pain or severe breathlessness. See `red-flags-when-urgent.md`.

## Questions for a clinician

- Should I have a blood test for anaemia?
- Could my periods be causing low iron?
- What foods should I eat more of?
- Do I need iron supplements, and for how long?
- When should my blood be checked again?
