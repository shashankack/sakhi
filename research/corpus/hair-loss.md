# Hair fall and scalp hair thinning

## Plain-language explanation

Losing some hair every day is normal; most people shed around 50–100 hairs a day. Hair fall becomes a concern when you notice:

- a **widening parting** or more scalp showing on the top or crown of the head
- a thinner ponytail than before
- much more hair than usual on the pillow, in the shower drain, or on your comb
- **patches** of complete hair loss

Patterns matter. **Gradual thinning on the top of the head** is different from **sudden heavy shedding all over** or **round bald patches**, and each points to different causes.

## PCOS education context

In some people with PCOS, higher androgen effects can cause **slow thinning on the top and crown of the scalp**, with the front hairline usually kept. This is sometimes called *female-pattern hair loss*. It is often discussed with PCOS because it can appear together with irregular periods, acne, or extra facial hair.

Hair thinning alone does **not** suggest PCOS. Female-pattern thinning also happens without PCOS, and many other causes are common and treatable.

This information is educational. It is not a diagnosis.

## Other possible causes and overlaps

- **Low iron / anaemia**: very common, especially with heavy periods
- **Thyroid problems**: both underactive and overactive thyroid
- **Telogen effluvium**: heavy shedding 2–3 months after a stressful event such as high fever, illness (including COVID), surgery, childbirth, crash dieting, or major emotional stress; usually recovers
- **After pregnancy**: shedding a few months after delivery is common and usually temporary
- **Alopecia areata**: round, smooth bald patches (an immune condition)
- **Tight hairstyles** (tight braids, buns, ponytails) pulling on the hairline
- Scalp conditions such as dandruff or fungal infection
- Low protein intake, some nutrient deficiencies
- Some medicines
- Family history

## Self-care notes

General hair-care ideas, not treatments:

- **Be gentle**: avoid very tight hairstyles, harsh chemical treatments, and lots of heat styling.
- Use a wide-tooth comb on wet hair.
- **Eat enough protein and iron-rich foods** (dal, rajma, chana, green leafy vegetables, eggs, meat or fish if you eat them).
- Avoid crash diets.
- Be cautious with expensive "hair regrowth" products and supplements with big promises. Discuss supplements with a clinician first, because too much of some vitamins can be harmful.
- Taking a monthly photo of your parting in the same light can help you see real change over time.

## Care pathway

- **Routine (next few weeks)**: gradual thinning or increased shedding. See a **GP** or **dermatologist**, who may check iron, thyroid, and hormone levels.
- **Soon (within 1–2 weeks)**: round bald patches, hair loss with scalp pain, redness or scaling, or hair loss together with **rapid** new facial hair growth or voice deepening.
- **Gynaecologist or endocrinologist**: if hair thinning comes with irregular periods, acne, or extra hair growth.

## Questions for a clinician

- What type of hair loss pattern do I have?
- Should my iron, thyroid, or hormone levels be checked?
- Could a recent illness, stress, or diet change explain the shedding?
- Could my hair thinning be related to my periods or other symptoms?
- What treatment options are there, and how long until I see results?
- Should I see a dermatologist?
