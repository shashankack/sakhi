# Care pathways: who to see and how soon

## Plain-language explanation

Knowing **who to see** and **how soon** makes it easier to get the right care. This file gives a simple guide. It is general information. A clinician who knows your situation may advise differently.

This corpus uses three urgency levels, matched to how the NHS groups its advice:

| Level | Meaning | Matches NHS advice to |
|-------|---------|------------------------|
| **Urgent** | Needs help now | call 999 or go to A&E (in India: dial **108** or go to an emergency department) |
| **Soon** | Should not wait for a routine visit | get an urgent GP appointment or contact NHS 111 |
| **Routine** | Worth checking, not time-sensitive | see a GP (non-urgent) |

If you are unsure, it is always reasonable to contact a GP or clinic and ask how soon you should be seen.

## PCOS education context

MedlinePlus says there is **no specific test** for PCOS. Clinicians usually piece together history, examination, and tests, and rule out other conditions. WHO says newly diagnosed women should have blood tests for insulin resistance and heart risk. The NHS says regular check-ups can reduce the risk of long-term conditions. See `pcos-long-term-health.md` and `tests-and-scans-explained.md`.

This information is educational. It is not a diagnosis.

## Who's who

- **General physician (GP) / family doctor**: a first stop for most concerns; can arrange basic tests and referrals.
- **Gynaecologist**: periods, ovaries, womb, contraception, pregnancy, and PCOS-related concerns.
- **Endocrinologist**: hormone conditions, such as thyroid, prolactin, and diabetes.
- **Dermatologist**: acne, unwanted hair, scalp hair loss, and skin patches.
- **Fertility specialist**: trying to conceive.
- **Dietitian**: eating plans for weight, blood sugar, and energy.
- **Psychiatrist, psychologist, or counsellor**: low mood, anxiety, body-image distress, and eating concerns.

## Quick guide by concern

| Concern | Usually | First stop |
|---------|---------|------------|
| Irregular periods, no other worries | Routine | GP / gynaecologist |
| Missed 3 periods in a row | Soon | GP / gynaecologist |
| Periods not started by age 15 | Soon | GP / paediatrician |
| Unwanted facial or body hair | Routine | GP / gynaecologist / dermatologist |
| Thick hair appearing suddenly, lower voice, bigger muscles | Soon | GP → endocrinologist |
| Acne not improving, or deep, painful or scarring | Routine | GP / dermatologist |
| Weight gain, dark skin patches on neck | Routine | GP / endocrinologist / dietitian |
| Very thirsty, peeing more, losing weight without trying | Soon | GP |
| Hair fall that worries you | Routine | GP / dermatologist |
| Tiredness affecting daily life | Routine | GP |
| Loud snoring, breathing pauses, very tired by day | Routine | GP |
| Period pain stopping normal activities | Routine | GP / gynaecologist |
| Severe period or pelvic pain not helped by painkillers | Soon | GP |
| Heavy periods affecting your life | Routine | GP / gynaecologist |
| Not pregnant after a year of trying (sooner if 36+) | Soon | GP / gynaecologist |
| Nipple discharge with headache or vision changes | Soon | GP → endocrinologist |
| Thoughts of self-harm | **Urgent** | Tele-MANAS 14416 / 1800-89-14416; 108 if harmed |
| Pelvic pain with heavy bleeding or feeling faint | **Urgent** | Emergency (108) |
| Severe or worsening pelvic or tummy pain | **Urgent** | Emergency (108) |
| Possible pregnancy with sudden severe pain or dizziness | **Urgent** | Emergency (108) |
| Chest pain or severe difficulty breathing | **Urgent** | Emergency (108) |

## Other possible causes and overlaps

Because many symptoms overlap, the first visit is often about **ruling things in or out**, not reaching a final answer in one appointment. See `overlap-thyroid-prolactin.md`.

## Self-care notes

How to prepare for an appointment:

- **Cycle record**: dates of your last few periods, how long they lasted, and any pain.
- **Symptom list**: what, since when, getting better or worse.
- **Medicines and supplements** you take, including herbal or ayurvedic products.
- **Family history**: diabetes, thyroid problems, PCOS.
- **Your top 2–3 questions** written down.
- It is okay to bring a trusted person with you, and to ask the clinician to explain again in simpler words.

## Care pathway

1. Check `red-flags-when-urgent.md`. If any apply, get urgent help first.
2. Otherwise, start with a **GP** or **gynaecologist**.
3. Expect possible blood tests and sometimes an ultrasound, chosen by the clinician. See `tests-and-scans-explained.md`.
4. You may be referred to a specialist depending on the findings.
5. Plan **follow-up** visits. Long-term conditions are looked after over time.

## Questions for a clinician

- Based on my symptoms, how soon should I be seen, and by whom?
- What tests do you suggest, and what will they tell us?
- When and how will I get my results?
- What signs should make me come back sooner?
