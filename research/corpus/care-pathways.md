# Care pathways: who to see and how soon

## Plain-language explanation

Knowing **who to see** and **how soon** makes it easier to get the right care. This file gives a simple guide. It is general information. A clinician who knows your situation may advise differently.

We use three urgency levels:

| Level | Meaning | Example timing |
|-------|---------|----------------|
| **Urgent** | Needs help now | Today: emergency department, **112** or **108** |
| **Soon** | Should not wait for a routine slot | Within days to about 2 weeks |
| **Routine** | Worth checking, not time-sensitive | Within the next few weeks |

If you are unsure, it is always reasonable to contact a GP or nurse and ask how soon you should be seen.

## PCOS education context

There is no single "PCOS test". Clinicians usually piece together history, examination, and tests, and rule out look-alike conditions. Long-term, PCOS care often involves a **team** over time. Regular follow-up for blood sugar, blood pressure, cholesterol, and mood can be part of looking after health.

This information is educational. It is not a diagnosis.

## Who's who

- **General physician (GP) / family doctor**: a good first stop for almost any concern; can arrange basic tests and referrals.
- **Gynaecologist**: periods, ovaries, womb, contraception, pregnancy, and PCOS-related concerns.
- **Endocrinologist**: hormone conditions such as thyroid, prolactin, adrenal, and diabetes.
- **Dermatologist**: acne, unwanted hair, scalp hair loss, and skin patches.
- **Fertility specialist / reproductive endocrinologist**: trying to conceive, ovulation support.
- **Dietitian / nutritionist (qualified)**: realistic eating plans for weight, blood sugar, and energy.
- **Psychiatrist, psychologist, or counsellor**: low mood, anxiety, body-image distress, eating concerns.
- **Government health centres** (PHC, CHC, district hospitals) and **ASHA workers** can also help with first steps and referrals in India.

## Quick guide by concern

| Concern | Usually | First stop |
|---------|---------|------------|
| Irregular periods, no other worries | Routine | GP / gynaecologist |
| No period 3+ months, pregnancy test negative | Soon | Gynaecologist |
| Unwanted facial/body hair, slowly increasing | Routine | GP / gynaecologist / dermatologist |
| Hair growth appearing rapidly, voice deepening | Soon | GP → endocrinologist |
| Persistent or scarring acne | Routine | Dermatologist |
| Weight gain, cravings, dark neck patches | Routine | GP / endocrinologist / dietitian |
| Extreme thirst, frequent urination, weight loss | Soon | GP |
| Hair thinning, gradual | Routine | GP / dermatologist |
| Round bald patches | Soon | Dermatologist |
| Tiredness affecting daily life | Routine | GP |
| Low mood most days for 2+ weeks | Soon | GP / mental health professional |
| Thoughts of self-harm | **Urgent** | Tele-MANAS 14416 / 112 |
| Trying to conceive 12 months (<35) / 6 months (35+), or irregular cycles | Soon | Gynaecologist |
| Milky nipple discharge with headache/vision change | Soon | GP → endocrinologist |
| Heavy bleeding with dizziness / fainting | **Urgent** | Emergency (112 / 108) |
| Severe pelvic or belly pain | **Urgent** | Emergency |
| Possible pregnancy + pain or bleeding | **Urgent** | Emergency |
| Chest pain or breathlessness | **Urgent** | Emergency (112 / 108) |

See `red-flags-when-urgent.md` for details on urgent symptoms.

## Other possible causes and overlaps

Because many symptoms overlap, the first visit is often about **ruling things in or out**, not about reaching a final answer in one appointment. See `overlap-thyroid-prolactin.md`.

## Self-care notes

How to prepare for an appointment:

- **Cycle record**: dates of your last 3–6 periods, heaviness, and pain.
- **Symptom list**: what, since when, getting better or worse.
- **Medicines and supplements** you take, including herbal or ayurvedic.
- **Family history**: diabetes, thyroid, PCOS, heart disease.
- **Your top 2–3 questions** written down.
- It is okay to bring a trusted person with you.
- It is okay to ask for a female doctor or a chaperone if that makes you more comfortable.
- It is okay to ask the clinician to explain again in simpler words.

## Care pathway

1. Check `red-flags-when-urgent.md`; if any apply, get urgent help first.
2. Otherwise, start with a **GP** or **gynaecologist**.
3. Expect possible blood tests (for example pregnancy, thyroid, prolactin, hormones, blood sugar) and sometimes an ultrasound, chosen by the clinician.
4. You may be referred to a specialist depending on findings.
5. Plan **follow-up** visits; long-term conditions are managed over time.

## Questions for a clinician

- Based on my symptoms, how soon should I be seen, and by whom?
- What tests do you suggest, and what will they tell us?
- When and how will I get my results?
- What signs should make me come back sooner?
- How often should I have follow-up checks?
