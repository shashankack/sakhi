# Red flags: when to get help urgently

## Plain-language explanation

Most period, skin, hair, and weight concerns can be checked at a routine appointment. Some symptoms, however, need **medical help straight away**. This file lists those warning signs, based on NHS emergency advice.

If any of these are happening **right now**, do not wait for an online answer. In India, **dial 108** for an emergency ambulance, or go to the nearest hospital emergency department. Do not drive yourself; ask someone to take you or call an ambulance.

## Red flag 1: Heavy bleeding with feeling faint or dizzy

The NHS says to call for emergency help when pelvic pain comes with:

- **heavy bleeding from the vagina**, or
- **feeling faint, dizzy or lightheaded, or passing out**

## Red flag 2: Severe pelvic or tummy pain

The NHS says to call for emergency help when pelvic pain:

- is **severe, getting worse**, or hurts when moving or touching the area
- comes with **pain in the tip of the shoulder**
- comes with **difficulty breathing**
- comes with **suddenly feeling confused**

The NHS also says to get urgent help for **sudden, severe pelvic pain**, or tummy pain with feeling sick or vomiting. These can be signs of a problem with an ovarian cyst.

## Red flag 3: Pregnancy concerns

**If you might be pregnant** (ectopic pregnancy warning signs), the NHS says to get emergency help for:

- a **sharp, sudden and intense pain** in your tummy
- feeling **very dizzy or fainting**
- **looking very pale**

Other ectopic pregnancy signs listed by the NHS are tummy pain low down on one side, vaginal bleeding or brown watery discharge, pain in the tip of the shoulder, and discomfort when peeing or pooing.

**If you are pregnant and bleeding**, the NHS says to get emergency help when there is:

- **severe pain in the tummy** (so bad it is hard to focus on daily tasks)
- **pain in the shoulder**
- **feeling sick, faint, or dizzy, or losing consciousness**
- **heavy bleeding** (soaking a period pad soon after putting it on)

## Red flag 4: Thoughts of self-harm or suicide

If you are thinking about hurting yourself or ending your life:

- **You are not alone, and help is available.**
- In India, call **Tele-MANAS** on **14416** or **1800-89-14416**. The Ministry of Health and Family Welfare lists these as toll-free numbers for 24x7 tele-mental health support.
- If someone has seriously harmed themselves, or feels they may be about to, get emergency help straight away (dial **108** or go to the nearest emergency department).
- The NHS says it is important to **tell someone** how you feel, to be around other people, and to get to a safe place, like a friend's house.

## Red flag 5: Chest pain or breathlessness

The NHS says to get emergency help for:

- **sudden pain or discomfort in the chest** that does not go away
- chest pain that **spreads** to the arm, neck, jaw, stomach, or back
- chest pain together with feeling **sweaty, sick, light-headed, or short of breath**
- **severe difficulty breathing**, pain in the chest or upper back, or a very fast heartbeat

## Other signs to check soon

These are not usually emergencies, but should be checked quickly:

- thick, dark hair appearing **suddenly**, a lower voice, or bigger muscles (NHS: urgent GP appointment)
- period pain or pelvic pain that is **severe or worse than usual** and painkillers have not helped (NHS: urgent GP or NHS 111)
- bleeding between periods or after sex
- missed 3 periods in a row
- feeling very thirsty, peeing more than usual, or losing weight without trying (possible signs of diabetes)

## PCOS education context

Having PCOS (now also called PMOS) or PCOS-like symptoms does **not** protect anyone from these emergencies. Red-flag symptoms should **never** be explained away as "just PCOS". When red flags are present, urgent care comes first.

This information is educational. It is not a diagnosis.

## Other possible causes and overlaps

Red-flag symptoms can have many causes. Only an in-person assessment can tell the difference. That is why urgent care is advised even when the cause is not clear.

## Self-care notes

- **Do not drive yourself** if you feel faint, are bleeding heavily, or are in severe pain.
- If possible, bring your phone, a list of medicines you take, and the date of your last period.

## Care pathway

- **Emergency now**: red flags 1, 2, 3, and 5 → dial **108** or go to the nearest emergency department.
- **Mental health crisis** → **Tele-MANAS 14416** or **1800-89-14416**; if someone has seriously harmed themselves, dial **108**.
- **Soon**: items under "Other signs to check soon" → GP, gynaecologist, or endocrinologist.

## Questions for a clinician

(For a follow-up visit after urgent symptoms have been handled.)

- What caused my symptoms, and could they happen again?
- What warning signs should make me come back immediately?
- Do I need any follow-up tests?
