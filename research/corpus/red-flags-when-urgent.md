# Red flags: when to get help urgently

## Plain-language explanation

Most period, skin, hair, and weight concerns can be checked at a routine appointment. Some symptoms, however, can be signs of something that needs **medical help today**. This file lists those warning signs.

If any of these are happening **right now**, do not wait for an online answer. **In India, call 112 (emergency) or 108 (ambulance)**, go to the nearest emergency department, or ask someone nearby to help you get there.

## Red flag 1: Very heavy bleeding with dizziness

Get emergency help if you have:

- soaking through a pad or tampon **every hour for 2 or more hours in a row**
- passing **large clots** (bigger than a large coin) repeatedly
- feeling **dizzy, light-headed, faint**, very weak, breathless, or with a racing heartbeat
- fainting or nearly fainting

Heavy bleeding can cause a dangerous loss of blood. It needs prompt care.

## Red flag 2: Severe pelvic or abdominal pain

Get emergency help if you have:

- **sudden, severe** pain in the lower belly or pelvis
- pain on **one side** that is severe, especially with **nausea or vomiting**
- severe pain with **fever**, chills, or unusual vaginal discharge
- pain so strong you cannot stand, walk, or talk normally

Causes can include a twisted ovary, a burst cyst, infection, appendicitis, or a pregnancy outside the womb. Some of these need treatment within hours.

## Red flag 3: Pregnancy concerns

- If you **might be pregnant** and have **belly pain, shoulder-tip pain, bleeding, dizziness, or fainting**, get **emergency** help. This can be a sign of a pregnancy outside the womb (ectopic pregnancy), which is an emergency.
- If you are **pregnant** and have heavy bleeding, severe pain, severe headache, vision changes, or swelling of the face and hands, get **urgent** help.
- A **positive pregnancy test** without these symptoms is not an emergency, but book a visit with a clinician **soon**.

## Red flag 4: Thoughts of self-harm or suicide

If you are thinking about hurting yourself or ending your life, or feel you cannot keep yourself safe:

- **You are not alone, and help is available right now.**
- In India, call **Tele-MANAS: 14416** or **1-800-891-4416** (free, 24×7, multiple languages).
- In immediate danger, call **112** or go to the nearest emergency department.
- Tell someone you trust (a friend, family member, or teacher) and ask them to stay with you.

Low mood linked with hormones, body changes, or fertility worries is real and treatable. Reaching out is a strong step.

## Red flag 5: Chest pain or breathlessness

Get emergency help (**112 / 108**) if you have:

- chest pain, pressure, or tightness, especially spreading to the arm, jaw, or back
- sudden shortness of breath or difficulty breathing
- coughing up blood
- a painful, swollen leg together with breathlessness (possible blood clot, especially for people using hormone medicines or after surgery)

## Other signs to check soon (within days)

These are not usually emergencies, but should not wait for a routine visit:

- hair growth, voice deepening, or muscle changes appearing **quickly** over a few months
- milky nipple discharge (when not breastfeeding) with **headaches or vision changes**
- no period for 3 months or more with a negative pregnancy test
- bleeding after sex or bleeding after menopause
- unexplained weight loss, extreme thirst, or passing urine very often

## PCOS education context

Having PCOS or PCOS-like symptoms does **not** protect anyone from these emergencies, and these red-flag symptoms should **never** be explained away as "just PCOS". When red flags are present, getting urgent care comes first. Education about PCOS can wait.

This information is educational. It is not a diagnosis.

## Other possible causes and overlaps

Red-flag symptoms can have many causes, some serious and some not. Only an in-person assessment can tell the difference. That is why urgent care is advised even when the cause is uncertain.

## Self-care notes

- **Do not drive yourself** if you feel faint, are bleeding heavily, or are in severe pain. Ask someone to take you or call an ambulance.
- If possible, bring: your phone, ID, a list of medicines you take, and the date of your last period.
- Do not take leftover or borrowed medicines to manage severe symptoms without medical advice.

## Care pathway

- **Emergency now**: any of red flags 1–5 above → call **112 / 108** or go to the nearest emergency department.
- **Mental health crisis** → **Tele-MANAS 14416** / **1-800-891-4416**, or 112 if in immediate danger.
- **Soon (within days)**: items under "Other signs to check soon" → GP, gynaecologist, or endocrinologist.

## Questions for a clinician

(For a follow-up visit after urgent symptoms have been handled.)

- What caused my symptoms, and could they happen again?
- What warning signs should make me come back immediately?
- Do I need any follow-up tests?
- Who should I contact if this happens at night or on a weekend?
