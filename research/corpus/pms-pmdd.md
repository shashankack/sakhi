# PMS and PMDD (changes before a period)

## Plain-language explanation

Many people notice body and mood changes in the **1–2 weeks before a period**. These usually ease within a few days after bleeding starts.

**PMS (premenstrual syndrome)** can include:

- mood swings, feeling irritable, low, or teary
- feeling anxious or tense
- tiredness and poor sleep
- bloating, sore breasts, headache
- food cravings or feeling hungrier
- spots or acne flare-ups

**PMDD (premenstrual dysphoric disorder)** is a less common, **more severe** form. Mood symptoms are strong enough to seriously affect work, study, or relationships every cycle. Some people feel hopeless or very angry, or have thoughts of self-harm, during this phase.

The key feature of both is **timing**: symptoms follow the cycle and get better after the period starts. They are thought to come from how the brain responds to normal hormone changes, not from "abnormal" hormone levels.

PMS and PMDD are **real** and are not "all in the head".

## PCOS education context

PMS and PMDD are **not** signs used to describe PCOS. They are separate topics.

People with PCOS may also have mood changes, anxiety, or low mood, but these do not always follow the cycle. When periods are irregular, it can be harder to see whether symptoms are premenstrual. A symptom diary helps tell these apart. See `mood-fatigue.md`.

This information is educational. It is not a diagnosis.

## Other possible causes and overlaps

- **Depression** or **anxiety** that is present most of the month but may get worse before periods
- **Thyroid problems**, which can cause tiredness and mood change
- **Anaemia** from heavy periods, causing tiredness
- **Perimenopause** (often in the 40s)
- **Stress, poor sleep**, or heavy workload
- Side effects of some medicines, including some hormonal contraception

## Self-care notes

- **Track symptoms daily for 2–3 cycles**: mood, sleep, energy, pain, and the day of your cycle. This is the most useful step for a clinician.
- **Regular exercise** (even a daily walk) has good evidence for easing PMS.
- **Calcium-rich foods** such as milk, curd, ragi, and sesame are among the better-studied food options.
- Try to keep **regular sleep** times.
- **Cut down** on caffeine, very salty food, and alcohol in the days before a period.
- Yoga, breathing exercises, or talking to someone you trust may help with stress.
- Tell close family or friends what helps you during these days.
- Do not start mood or hormone medicines on your own.

## Care pathway

- **Routine (book in the next few weeks)**: symptoms that bother you each month but are manageable.
- **Soon (within 1–2 weeks)**: symptoms that disrupt work, study, or relationships every month; strong feelings of hopelessness, anger, or anxiety before periods; or low mood that does not lift after the period.
- **Urgent (same day / emergency)**: any thoughts of self-harm or suicide, at any point in the cycle. In India, call **Tele-MANAS 14416** or **1-800-891-4416**, or emergency **112**. See `red-flags-when-urgent.md`.

Who to see: a **GP** or **gynaecologist**. A **psychiatrist** or **counsellor** can help with mood symptoms.

## Questions for a clinician

- Do my symptoms follow my cycle in a way that fits PMS or PMDD?
- Could depression, anxiety, thyroid, or anaemia be part of it?
- What lifestyle changes are most worth trying first?
- What treatment options exist if self-care is not enough?
- Should I see a mental health professional?
- How should I track my symptoms before the next visit?
