# PMS and PMDD (changes before a period)

## Plain-language explanation

The NHS describes **PMS (premenstrual syndrome)** as the name for the symptoms women can have **in the weeks before their period**.

The NHS lists symptoms such as:

- mood swings
- feeling low, irritable, or anxious
- tiredness or trouble sleeping
- bloating or cramps
- breast tenderness
- headaches
- skin and hair changes
- changes in appetite or food cravings

The NHS says a small number of women have more severe symptoms, called **PMDD (premenstrual dysphoric disorder)**. Its symptoms are more intense and affect daily life more. The NHS says the cause is not clear, but it has been linked to sensitivity to hormone changes and to inherited genes.

## PCOS education context

PMS and PMDD are **not** features used to diagnose PCOS (now also called PMOS). They are separate topics.

People with PCOS may also have depression or anxiety, which WHO links with PCOS. These do not always follow the cycle. When periods are irregular, it can be harder to tell whether symptoms are premenstrual. A symptom diary helps. See `mood-fatigue.md`.

This information is educational. It is not a diagnosis.

## Other possible causes and overlaps

- **Depression or anxiety** present most of the month. See `mood-fatigue.md`.
- **Thyroid problems**: the NHS lists tiredness (underactive thyroid) and anxiety and irritability (overactive thyroid).
- **Iron deficiency anaemia**, which can cause tiredness. See `anaemia-and-periods.md`.
- **Stress** and poor sleep. See `sleep-stress-pcos.md`.

## Self-care notes

The NHS suggests:

- **exercise regularly**
- **eat a healthy, balanced diet**. Eating smaller meals every 2 to 3 hours may help.
- **get plenty of sleep**
- try **reducing stress**, for example with yoga or meditation
- painkillers from a pharmacy for pain. Ask a pharmacist which one is safe for you.
- keep a **symptom diary for at least 2 cycles**

The NHS says **not** to smoke or drink too much alcohol.

Do not start mood or hormone medicines on your own.

## Care pathway

The NHS advises seeing a GP if lifestyle changes have not helped, or symptoms are affecting daily life.

- **Routine**: symptoms that bother you each month.
- **Soon**: symptoms that disrupt work, study, or relationships every month.
- **Urgent (now)**: the NHS says anyone with PMDD symptoms who is feeling suicidal should get emergency help. In India, call **Tele-MANAS 14416** or **1800-89-14416**, or dial **108** if someone has seriously harmed themselves. See `red-flags-when-urgent.md`.

Who to see: a **GP** or **gynaecologist**. A **psychiatrist** or **counsellor** can help with mood symptoms.

## Questions for a clinician

- Do my symptoms follow my cycle in a way that fits PMS or PMDD?
- Could depression, anxiety, thyroid, or anaemia be part of it?
- What lifestyle changes are most worth trying first?
- What treatment options exist if self-care is not enough?
- How should I track my symptoms before the next visit?
