# Tests and scans explained

## Plain-language explanation

When someone has symptoms that might be linked to PCOS (now also called PMOS), a clinician may suggest some tests. MedlinePlus says there is **no specific test for PCOS**. Tests help build a picture and **rule out other causes**.

Tests that trusted sources mention:

- **Physical examination**: MedlinePlus says a provider will look for physical signs, such as extra hair growth.
- **Pelvic examination**: MedlinePlus says this checks for signs of extra male hormones and whether the ovaries are enlarged or swollen.
- **Blood tests for hormones**: the NHS lists blood tests to check hormone levels and insulin resistance.
- **Testosterone**: WHO lists high testosterone on a blood test as one way to show high androgens.
- **AMH (anti-Müllerian hormone)**: the 2023 international guideline says AMH could be used instead of an ultrasound in **adults**, but should **not yet** be used in teenagers.
- **Thyroid test (TSH and T4)**: the NHS says these check for thyroid problems. See `overlap-thyroid-prolactin.md`.
- **Prolactin test**: MedlinePlus says this measures prolactin in a blood sample, but cannot by itself show the cause of a high level.
- **Blood sugar tests**: WHO says newly diagnosed women should have blood tests for insulin resistance and heart risk. The 2023 guideline says an **oral glucose tolerance test (OGTT)** should be offered when planning pregnancy.
- **Full blood count (FBC)**: the NHS says this is usually used to check for iron deficiency anaemia. See `anaemia-and-periods.md`.
- **Pregnancy test**: a missed period is often an early sign of pregnancy (NHS).

## PCOS education context

**Ultrasound scan**: the NHS says an ultrasound uses sound waves to make images of the inside of the body, and does **not use any radiation**. There are two types:

- **external**: a probe is moved across the skin of the tummy. The NHS says this does not usually cause pain. The clinic may ask you to drink water first to fill the bladder.
- **internal (transvaginal)**: a thin probe is placed inside the vagina. It can be uncomfortable; the NHS advises telling the person doing the scan if you feel pain.

The NHS says an ultrasound for PMOS is **only done if you are over 18**. WHO says ovarian cysts are **not required** for a PCOS diagnosis.

No single test result means someone has PCOS. A clinician looks at symptoms and results together.

This information is educational. It is not a diagnosis.

## Other possible causes and overlaps

Tests are often used to check for other conditions with similar symptoms, such as:

- pregnancy
- thyroid problems
- high prolactin
- iron deficiency anaemia
- diabetes

## Self-care notes

- Ask **why** each test is being done and what it will show.
- Ask whether you need to **prepare**, for example by fasting or having a full bladder.
- Keep copies of your reports in one folder.
- Bring your cycle diary. The date of your last period can matter for some tests.

## Care pathway

- **Routine**: tests are usually arranged by a **GP**, **gynaecologist**, or **endocrinologist**.
- **Soon**: ask for results to be explained if a report worries you.
- **Urgent**: tests should never delay urgent care. See `red-flags-when-urgent.md`.

## Questions for a clinician

- Which tests do I need, and why?
- Do I need to prepare in any way?
- When and how will I get my results?
- What do my results mean, in simple words?
- Do any tests need to be repeated?
