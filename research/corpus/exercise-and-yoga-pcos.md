# Exercise, yoga and PCOS

## Plain-language explanation

Being active is good for almost everyone. WHO lists benefits for adults including **better mental health, thinking skills, sleep**, and measures of body fat.

How much activity is recommended:

- **Adults**: the 2023 international PCOS guideline says adults (18 to 64) should aim for **at least 150 to 300 minutes of moderate activity a week**, plus **muscle-strengthening on 2 non-consecutive days** a week. The NHS gives a similar target: at least **150 minutes of moderate** activity or **75 minutes of vigorous** activity a week, and strengthening on at least 2 days.
- **Teenagers**: the 2023 guideline says teenagers should aim for **at least 60 minutes of moderate to vigorous activity a day**.
- ICMR-NIN says a **minimum 30 to 45 minutes of brisk walking** or moderate activity among adults helps maintain good health.

## PCOS education context

WHO recommends **physical activity** for all women with PCOS (now also called PMOS), **even without weight loss**. WHO links PCOS with insulin resistance and type 2 diabetes, and ICMR-NIN says physical activity controls body weight, reduces fat mass, and increases muscle mass.

**Yoga:**
- The NHS lists **yoga**, pilates, and tai chi as **muscle-strengthening** activities.
- The NHS suggests gentle exercise such as **yoga** to help with **period pain**. See `painful-periods.md`.
- The NHS suggests **yoga or meditation** to reduce stress with **PMS**. See `pms-pmdd.md`.

No trusted source opened for this file says yoga cures or treats PCOS itself.

This information is educational. It is not a diagnosis.

## Other possible causes and overlaps

- Exercising **too much** can itself cause irregular or missed periods (NHS). See `cycles-irregular.md`.
- Feeling breathless or very tired with light activity can be a sign of **anaemia**. See `anaemia-and-periods.md`.

## Self-care notes

The NHS lists these as **moderate** activities: brisk walking, water aerobics, cycling, dancing, doubles tennis, pushing a lawn mower, hiking, and rollerblading.

The NHS also advises:

- cutting down on time spent **sitting or lying down**
- breaking up long periods of not moving with some activity

Tips:

- Start small, for example a 10-minute walk, and build up.
- Pick activities you enjoy, such as dancing, walking with a friend, yoga, or sport.
- The NHS says some people find exercising close to bedtime affects sleep, so try being active earlier in the day.

## Care pathway

- **Routine**: ask a GP before starting if there is a heart, lung, or joint condition, or a pregnancy.
- **Soon**: breathlessness, dizziness, or chest discomfort during activity should be checked.
- **Urgent**: chest pain spreading to the arm, neck, or jaw, or with sweating or breathlessness. See `red-flags-when-urgent.md`.

## Questions for a clinician

- How much activity is safe for me to start with?
- What type of exercise would suit my health and my symptoms?
- Is there anything I should avoid?
- Can you refer me to someone who can help me plan activity?
