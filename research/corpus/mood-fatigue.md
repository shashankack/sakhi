# Mood changes, anxiety, and tiredness

## Plain-language explanation

Feeling low, anxious, irritable, or tired all the time can affect daily life as much as any physical symptom. These feelings are **real**, and worth taking seriously.

**Fatigue** means tiredness that does not go away with normal rest.

## PCOS education context

WHO says PCOS is linked with **anxiety, depression, eating disorders, and negative body image**. The NHS lists depression or anxiety, and feeling very tired, among the symptoms of PMOS (the new name for PCOS).

The 2023 international guideline says clinicians should **screen for depression** in all adults and teenagers with PCOS, and for **anxiety** in all adults with PCOS.

The guideline also says women with PCOS have a **higher rate of obstructive sleep apnoea** than women without PCOS, and should be asked about its symptoms. The NHS lists sleep apnoea symptoms as loud snoring, breathing stopping and starting during sleep, waking up a lot, and feeling very tired during the day.

These are associations. Low mood or tiredness **on its own does not suggest PCOS**, and mental health deserves care whatever the cause.

This information is educational. It is not a diagnosis.

## Other possible causes and overlaps

For tiredness:
- **Iron deficiency anaemia**: the NHS lists tiredness and lack of energy, shortness of breath, heart palpitations, and paler skin. Heavy periods are a very common cause. See `anaemia-and-periods.md`.
- **Underactive thyroid**: the NHS lists feeling extremely tired and feeling cold more than usual.
- **Type 2 diabetes**: the NHS lists feeling very tired among common symptoms.
- **Sleep apnoea** (see above).

For mood:
- **PMS and PMDD**: mood changes in the weeks before a period. See `pms-pmdd.md`.
- **Overactive thyroid**: the NHS lists nervousness, anxiety, and irritability.
- Stress and life events. See `sleep-stress-pcos.md`.

## Self-care notes

General wellbeing ideas, not treatment:

- **Talk to someone you trust** about how you feel. The NHS suggests talking therapy for depression or anxiety with PMOS.
- **Be active**: WHO lists improved mental health and sleep among the benefits of physical activity.
- **Keep regular sleep times.** See `sleep-stress-pcos.md`.
- **Notice patterns**: does your mood or energy change with your cycle? Note it in a diary.
- See `body-image-and-emotional-health.md`.

## Care pathway

- **Routine**: ongoing tiredness, low mood, or anxiety affecting daily life. A **GP** can check for causes such as anaemia or thyroid problems.
- **Soon**: loud snoring with breathing pauses and daytime tiredness (the NHS advises seeing a GP); low mood that does not lift.
- **Urgent (now)**: thoughts of self-harm or suicide → **Tele-MANAS 14416** or **1800-89-14416**; dial **108** if someone has seriously harmed themselves. See `red-flags-when-urgent.md`.

## Questions for a clinician

- Could my tiredness be from anaemia, thyroid, blood sugar, or sleep problems?
- Should I have blood tests?
- Could my mood be connected to hormones or my cycle?
- What support is available for anxiety or low mood, such as talking therapy?
- Could I have sleep apnoea, given my snoring?
