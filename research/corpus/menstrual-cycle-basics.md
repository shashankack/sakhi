# Menstrual cycle basics

## Plain-language explanation

The NHS says a **period** is the part of the menstrual cycle when a woman bleeds from her vagina for a few days.

The **menstrual cycle** is counted from the first day of one period to the first day of the next. The NHS gives these typical numbers:

- the average cycle is **around 28 days**
- periods usually come **every 21 to 35 days**
- a period can last **between 2 and 7 days**, but usually lasts **about 5 days**
- most people lose **about 20 to 90ml** of blood (about 1 to 5 tablespoons) during a period, though some lose more

The NHS says periods usually start around the age of 12, and most girls have regular periods by **16 to 18**.

**Ovulation** is the monthly release of an egg from an ovary (NHS).

## PCOS education context

Irregular or absent periods are one of the three features used to describe PCOS (now also called PMOS). The NHS says that in PMOS the ovaries may not release an egg each month.

Knowing your usual cycle pattern helps you notice changes and describe them to a clinician. A cycle outside the usual range does **not** mean someone has PCOS. See `cycles-irregular.md`.

This information is educational. It is not a diagnosis.

## Other possible causes and overlaps

The NHS lists these causes of irregular periods:

- puberty and the start of menopause (usually between 45 and 55)
- pregnancy
- hormonal contraception
- losing or gaining a lot of weight
- stress and anxiety
- exercising too much

## Self-care notes

- **Track your cycle**: note the first day of each period and how many days you bleed. A calendar or app works.
- Note anything unusual, such as bleeding between periods or after sex.
- The NHS lists pads, tampons, menstrual cups, and period underwear as period products. See `period-hygiene-products.md`.

## Care pathway

The NHS advises getting periods checked if:

- your periods change, for example becoming longer or lighter
- you bleed between periods, after sex, or after menopause
- 3 periods in a row are missed and a pregnancy test is negative

- **Routine**: periods that are irregular, or last longer than 7 days.
- **Soon**: missed 3 periods in a row; bleeding between periods or after sex.
- **Urgent**: heavy bleeding with feeling faint, or severe pelvic pain. See `red-flags-when-urgent.md`.

## Questions for a clinician

- Is my cycle length within the usual range?
- Is the amount I bleed normal?
- What changes in my periods should I watch for?
- What should I write in my period diary?
