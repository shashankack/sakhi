# Body image and emotional health

## Plain-language explanation

**Body image** is how a person sees and feels about their own body. Changes like extra facial hair, acne, hair loss, or weight gain can affect how someone feels about themselves, their confidence, and their relationships.

These feelings are **valid**, and support is available.

## PCOS education context

The 2023 international guideline says:

- clinicians should be aware that **features of PCOS can have a negative impact on body image**
- **eating disorders and disordered eating** should be considered in PCOS, **regardless of weight**
- clinicians should **screen for depression** in all adults and teenagers with PCOS, and for **anxiety** in all adults with PCOS

WHO links PCOS (now also called PMOS) with **anxiety, depression, eating disorders, and negative body image**.

The NHS suggests considering **talking therapy** for depression or anxiety with PMOS. For acne, the NHS lists "affecting your mental health" as a reason to see a GP.

These are associations. Feeling low about your body does **not** mean someone has PCOS.

This information is educational. It is not a diagnosis.

## Other possible causes and overlaps

- **Depression and anxiety** from other causes. See `mood-fatigue.md`.
- **PMS and PMDD**: mood changes before a period. See `pms-pmdd.md`.
- **Stress** and life pressures. See `sleep-stress-pcos.md`.

## Self-care notes

- **Talk to someone you trust** about how you feel.
- Remember that many visible changes can be managed. See `hirsutism-acne.md` and `hair-loss.md`.
- Limit comparing yourself with edited images on social media.
- Be active in ways you enjoy. WHO lists better mental health among the benefits of physical activity.
- Avoid crash diets or extreme food rules. Ask for help if eating feels out of control.
- Families: avoid comments about weight, skin, or hair. Kind support helps more than criticism.

## Care pathway

- **Routine**: body image worries affecting confidence or daily life → **GP**, **counsellor**, or **psychologist**.
- **Soon**: eating patterns that feel out of control; low mood or anxiety most days.
- **Urgent (now)**: thoughts of self-harm or suicide → **Tele-MANAS 14416** or **1800-89-14416** (toll-free, 24x7); dial **108** if someone has seriously harmed themselves. The NHS says it is important to **tell someone** how you feel. See `red-flags-when-urgent.md`.

## Questions for a clinician

- Could my low mood or anxiety be linked to my symptoms?
- What support is available, such as counselling or talking therapy?
- What can be done about the changes that bother me most?
- Can you refer me to someone who helps with eating concerns?
