# Indian food and PCOS

## Plain-language explanation

There is **no single "PCOS diet"**. The 2023 international PCOS guideline says there is **no evidence to support any one type of diet** over another for weight-related outcomes in PCOS.

WHO recommends **healthy eating** for all women with PCOS (now also called PMOS), **even without weight loss**.

For Indian food, the **ICMR-National Institute of Nutrition (ICMR-NIN)** published the **Dietary Guidelines for Indians 2024**. Its main messages include:

- **Eat a variety of foods** for a balanced diet. No single food has all the nutrients.
- In ICMR-NIN's "My Plate for the Day", **vegetables, fruits, green leafy vegetables, tubers, and roots** make up about **half the plate**.
- **At least half of cereals** should be **whole grains**, such as millets.
- **Cereals and millets** should give **not more than 45%** of daily energy.
- **Pair cereals or millets with pulses or beans** for good-quality protein and fibre.
- **Restrict cooking oil** to 25g to 30g per day.
- **Avoid sugar**, or limit it to 20g to 25g per day for adults.
- **Avoid ultra-processed foods** and foods high in fat, sugar, and salt.
- **Restrict salt.**
- **Drink enough water.**

## PCOS education context

WHO links PCOS with **insulin resistance, type 2 diabetes, and obesity**. Balanced eating is part of looking after long-term health. See `weight-insulin.md` and `pcos-long-term-health.md`.

The 2023 guideline says **eating disorders and disordered eating** should be considered in PCOS, **regardless of weight**. Food advice should never become shaming or extreme.

This information is educational. It is not a diagnosis.

## Other possible causes and overlaps

- **Iron deficiency anaemia** is linked to diet and heavy periods. See `anaemia-and-periods.md`.
- **Type 2 diabetes** can occur with or without PCOS. The NHS says risk is higher for people over 25 with a South Asian background.
- **Thyroid problems** can affect weight (NHS).

## Self-care notes

Ideas based on ICMR-NIN guidance:

- Fill about **half your plate** with sabzi, salad, greens, and fruit.
- Choose **whole grains and millets** more often (for example ragi or bajra, or minimally polished grains).
- Have **dal, chana, rajma, or other pulses** with your rice or roti.
- Cut down on **sweets, sugary drinks**, and **packaged or deep-fried snacks**.
- For iron: ICMR-NIN says **green leafy vegetables, pulses, and dry fruits** contain iron, and **vitamin C-rich fruits** like guava, pineapple, lemon, and orange help the body absorb it.
- ICMR-NIN advises **avoiding tea** before, during, or soon after a meal, because tea binds iron.
- Be careful with "PCOS cure" powders and teas. WHO and the NHS say there is no cure.

## Care pathway

- **Routine**: a **dietitian** can make a personal plan that fits your family's cooking and budget.
- **Soon**: feeling very thirsty, peeing more than usual, or losing weight without trying (possible signs of diabetes); eating patterns that feel out of control.
- **Urgent**: see `red-flags-when-urgent.md`.

## Questions for a clinician

- What would a balanced plate look like for me?
- Should I have my blood sugar or iron checked?
- How can I change my diet without giving up foods my family eats?
- Can you refer me to a dietitian?
- Are there any supplements I should avoid?
