# Lifestyle and PCOS: an overview

## Plain-language explanation

WHO recommends **healthy lifestyle behaviours, including healthy eating and physical activity**, for **all** women with PCOS (now also called PMOS), **even without weight loss**.

The NHS lifestyle advice for PMOS is to eat a healthy, balanced diet, exercise regularly, and try to lose weight if overweight.

This file is a short overview. For details, see:

- food: `indian-diet-pcos.md`
- movement: `exercise-and-yoga-pcos.md`
- sleep and stress: `sleep-stress-pcos.md`

## PCOS education context

WHO links PCOS with **insulin resistance, type 2 diabetes, and obesity**. This is one reason food and movement are part of PCOS care. See `weight-insulin.md`.

The 2023 international guideline says there is **no evidence to support any one type of diet** over another for weight-related outcomes in PCOS. A balanced pattern that suits the person, their culture, and their budget is the goal.

The guideline also says **eating disorders and disordered eating** should be considered in PCOS, **regardless of weight**.

This information is educational. It is not a diagnosis.

## Other possible causes and overlaps

- **Underactive thyroid** can cause weight gain and tiredness (NHS).
- **Type 2 diabetes** can cause tiredness, thirst, and peeing more (NHS).
- **Iron deficiency anaemia** can cause tiredness (NHS). See `anaemia-and-periods.md`.
- **Some medicines** can affect weight or skin.

## Self-care notes

- **Food**: ICMR-NIN advises a balanced diet with a variety of foods, with vegetables, fruits, and greens making up about half the plate. See `indian-diet-pcos.md`.
- **Movement**: the 2023 guideline says adults should aim for **150 to 300 minutes** of moderate activity a week, plus muscle-strengthening on **2 non-consecutive days**. See `exercise-and-yoga-pcos.md`.
- **Sleep**: the NHS says most healthy adults need **around 7 to 9 hours** of sleep a night. See `sleep-stress-pcos.md`.
- **Be careful with** "miracle" supplements, teas, or powders that claim to cure PCOS. WHO and the NHS both say there is no cure.
- Avoid crash diets.

## Care pathway

- **Routine**: wanting help to plan food and exercise, or a check of weight and blood sugar.
- **Soon**: feeling very thirsty, peeing more than usual, or losing weight without trying (possible signs of diabetes); eating patterns that feel out of control.
- **Urgent**: fainting, chest pain, or thoughts of self-harm. See `red-flags-when-urgent.md`.

Who to see: a **GP**, **gynaecologist**, or **endocrinologist**. A **dietitian** can make a personal food plan.

## Questions for a clinician

- Should I have tests for blood sugar, cholesterol, or thyroid?
- What kind of food plan would suit me and my family's cooking?
- How much exercise should I aim for, and what type?
- Can you refer me to a dietitian?
- Are there any supplements I should avoid?
