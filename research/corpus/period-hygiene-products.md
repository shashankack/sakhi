# Period products and hygiene

## Plain-language explanation

The NHS lists the main types of period products as:

- **pads** (also called towels), which can be disposable or reusable
- **tampons**
- **menstrual cups**
- **period underwear**, which is absorbent and washable

The NHS says all of these are **safe and suitable**, even for a first period. It suggests **pads** at first, since tampons and cups can take some getting used to.

The right product is a personal choice, based on comfort, cost, and what is available.

## PCOS education context

Period products do not cause or treat PCOS (now also called PMOS). But they matter for people whose periods are **heavy, long, or unpredictable**:

- The NHS says periods may be heavy if you need to change your pad or tampon **every 1 to 2 hours**, or periods last **more than 7 days**. See `heavy-periods.md`.
- When periods are irregular, it can help to keep a product with you.

This information is educational. It is not a diagnosis.

## Other possible causes and overlaps

**Toxic shock syndrome (TSS)**: the NHS describes TSS as a **rare but life-threatening** condition caused by an infection. The NHS lists using **tampons or menstrual cups** as one way it can happen.

The NHS lists TSS symptoms including:
- a high temperature
- feeling hot, cold, or shivery
- muscle aches
- a rash that feels like sandpaper
- flu-like symptoms

The NHS says symptoms develop quickly and need **urgent treatment**.

## Self-care notes

From NHS advice on TSS:

- **Wash your hands** and **follow the instructions** when using tampons or menstrual cups.
- Do not leave tampons or cups in **longer than needed or recommended**.

General tips:

- Change products regularly and as instructed on the pack.
- Reusable pads and cups should be cleaned as the maker instructs.
- Keep a record of how often you need to change products. This helps a clinician judge whether bleeding is heavy.

## Care pathway

- **Routine**: questions about which product to use; periods that feel heavy.
- **Soon**: needing to change products every 1 to 2 hours, or periods lasting more than 7 days. See `heavy-periods.md`.
- **Urgent (emergency)**: the NHS says to get emergency help for TSS signs such as **confusion, blue or pale skin, or difficulty breathing**, especially while using a tampon or cup. Do not drive yourself; ask someone to take you or dial **108**. See `red-flags-when-urgent.md`.

## Questions for a clinician

- Which period product is best for me?
- Is it safe for me to use a menstrual cup or tampon?
- How often should I change my product?
- Does the amount I bleed sound normal?
