# Menstrual cycle changes

Cycles that come very late, skip months, or feel unpredictable are a common reason people seek answers. A typical adult cycle is often roughly 21–35 days, but stress, travel, weight change, illness, and hormonal patterns can shift timing.

## PCOS education context

In PCOS education, irregular or infrequent periods are often discussed as one possible pattern linked to irregular ovulation. Clinicians use history, exam, and tests — never a symptom checklist alone.

## Other possible causes

Thyroid problems, high prolactin, pregnancy, perimenopause, certain medicines, and intense stress can also change cycles.

## Self-care notes

- Note start dates of the last 3–6 periods.
- Write down flow changes, pain, and new medicines or weight shifts.
- Do not treat online cycle calculators as medical answers.

## Care pathway

Book a routine visit with a GP or gynecologist if cycles stay irregular for several months, or sooner if trying to conceive.

## Questions for a clinician

- Could my cycle pattern relate to ovulation changes?
- Which blood tests or ultrasound, if any, make sense for me?
- How should we rule out thyroid or other hormonal causes?
